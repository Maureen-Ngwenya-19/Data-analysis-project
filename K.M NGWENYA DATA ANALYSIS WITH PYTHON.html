b. Handling Missing Values in Both Datasets

STEP 2 : Handling Missing Values
Handling Service Delivery Protest Data
Number of Protests
  • The median is a measure of central tendency used to identify the middle value in a dataset after the values have been arranged from smallest to largest.
  • It is less affected by extremely small or large values, known as outliers.
  • Therefore, it may be suitable for handling missing values in the Number of Protests column because the mean can be significantly affected by unusually high protest counts.
  • These extreme values can make the data distribution skewed, whereas the median is more resistant to their influence.
Municipality
  • For the Municipality column, we will use the placeholder "Unknown" to fill in missing values because this is a categorical column containing text values.
  • This approach preserves the affected records instead of removing them and clearly indicates that the municipality information is unavailable.

Protest Category

  • For the Protest Category column, we will use the placeholder "Unknown" to fill in missing values because this is a categorical column containing text values.
  • This approach preserves the affected records instead of removing them and clearly indicates that the protest category information is unavailable.

Estimated Participants

  • The median is a measure of central tendency used to identify the middle value in a dataset after the values have been arranged from smallest to largest.
  • The median is suitable for the Estimated Participants column because the number of participants may vary significantly between provinces.
  • Some provinces may experience protests with very high attendance, while others may have protests with relatively few participants. These extreme differences can affect the mean, whereas the median is less affected by unusually high or low values.

Handling Basic Service Data
Total Households
  • We will use the mean to fill in the missing values in the Total Households column because only two values are missing from the dataset.

  • This is because the datasets record different information.
  • For example, a protest record may contain a protest category, such as sanitation or electricity, but may not have a matching household access record for that particular province or period.
  • Similarly, some household records may not have corresponding protest records. Therefore, these missing values are a result of unmatched records during data integration and do not necessarily indicate that the original data cleaning was unsuccessful.

Question 1.7

The visualisations provide valuable insights into the relationship between access to basic services and service delivery protests across South Africa. The analysis shows that Limpopo, Eastern Cape and North West recorded the highest protest rates per 100,000 households, while also having the lowest levels of access to basic services. In contrast, Western Cape and Gauteng had higher service access rates and considerably lower protest rates. This suggests that communities with limited access to essential services may experience greater dissatisfaction with service delivery.

The analysis also shows that access to basic services improved across all nine provinces between 2019 and 2023. Free State recorded the largest improvement of 7.0 percentage points, while Northern Cape recorded the smallest improvement of 5.1 percentage points. However, the differences between provinces remain, indicating that improvements are not happening equally.

Furthermore, protest activity increased during the second half of 2023, with July recording the highest number of protests at 184. The scatter plot revealed a strong negative correlation of -0.98, indicating that provinces with lower access to basic services generally experience higher protest rates.

Based on these findings, Siyahamba's management is not relevant here; municipal officials and government departments should prioritise improving water, electricity and sanitation services in provinces with lower access rates. They should also monitor protest trends, identify communities experiencing service delivery challenges and allocate resources according to their needs. Regular monitoring and evaluation can help measure progress and identify areas requiring further intervention.

Overall, the visualisations demonstrate how data analysis can support informed decision-making, improve service delivery planning and help government identify inequalities in access to essential services. However, correlation does not prove causation, as other factors such as unemployment, poverty, housing shortages and local governance may also contribute to service delivery protests.
